About the Challenge

The RF, Microwave and Wireless Design Competition is designed to bridge academic learning with industry-style RF product development. The competition is open to undergraduate students only. Participants will work through the full engineering cycle of concept development, electromagnetic simulation, fabrication, testing, and validation.

The competition emphasizes predictive engineering—rewarding teams that not only build a working RF system, but also accurately correlate measured hardware performance with simulated results. The competition focuses on the design and validation of a high-isolation self-diplexing antenna for Wi-Fi 6 applications, requiring participants to create a compact dual-band planar antenna capable of separating the 2.45 GHz and 5.825 GHz Wi-Fi bands into dedicated physical channels.

Challenge Topic

Project Theme

Design and validation of a high-isolation self-diplexing antenna for Wi-Fi 6 applications.

System Architecture

A single-radiator, dual-port planar self-diplexing antenna that separates the two primary Wi-Fi 6 operating bands into dedicated physical channels without relying on external diplexer or filter hardware.

Band Allocation

Port 1: 2.45 GHz ISM band
Port 2: 5.825 GHz UNII band

Feed Architectures

Participants may use integrated planar microstrip, CPW, slot-line, or coaxial probe feed excitation architectures optimized for mutual decoupling and high isolation.

Participation & Prize Pool

Team Size: 3–4 Members

Participants must register in teams of 3–4 members. The competition is open to undergraduate students enrolled in a college or university program. Teams are encouraged to distribute responsibilities across antenna design, simulation, fabrication, and testing for a well-rounded engineering workflow.

Every registered participant will receive a Participation Certificate in recognition of their contribution to the competition.

Technical Specifications

Electrical & RF Targets

  • Port 1 centered around 2.45 GHz
  • Port 2 centered around 5.825 GHz
  • Operating bandwidth of at least 100 MHz for each band
  • Strong impedance matching and high port-to-port isolation
  • Target peak gain at boresight radiation angle
  • Standard 50 Ω RF termination

Mechanical & Material Constraints

  • Maximum physical board area: ≤ 25 cm²
  • Substrate material: FR-4
  • Permitted FR-4 thicknesses include 0.8 mm, 1 mm, 1.2 mm, or 1.6 mm
  • Connector type: 2 × high-frequency PCB SMA female connectors
  • Copper thickness: 35 µm or 18 µm

Simulation Tool Constraints

  • Approved tools include HFSS, CST, ADS, AWR, FEKO, and equivalent 3D EM suites
  • Only academic, research, or student licenses are permitted

Manufacturing & Verification Tolerances

  • Dimensional tolerance target: ±0.5 mm
  • Substrate permittivity variation: Δεr = ±0.1
  • Target Sim-to-Meas delta: ≤ 5% frequency shift and ≤ 3 dB isolation degradation

Competition Timeline

At the end of Phase 1, all submissions will be evaluated and a shortlist of selected teams will be announced. Only the shortlisted teams will advance to Phase 2, where they will fabricate and experimentally validate their proposed design.

Phase 1

Design & Simulation

August - October 2026

Evaluation Rubrics

40% — Defense, Craftsmanship & Novelty

  • Design novelty and innovation
  • Soldering precision
  • Drilling accuracy
  • Jury Q&A technical defense

20% — RF Performance

  • Impedance matching (S11 / S22)
  • Self-diplexing isolation (S21 / S12)
  • Far-field radiation peak gain

20% — Sim-to-Meas Correlation

  • Frequency shift delta (Δf)
  • Isolation trace correlation (ΔS21)
  • Root-cause deviation analysis

20% — Technical Documentation

  • Simulation report and Gerber completeness
  • Fabrication, testing, and BOM report quality
  • Clarity of plots, overlays, and engineering documentation

Fabrication & Verification

Teams are expected to physically realize their antenna designs using standard PCB prototyping workflows such as milling, etching, drilling, and connector mounting. Particular emphasis will be placed on board precision, SMA mounting quality, alignment, soldering cleanliness, and robustness against practical manufacturing tolerances.

Final validation will be carried out using calibrated VNA measurements, and teams will be expected to explain the correlation between their simulated and measured performance. Successful entries will demonstrate both strong RF behavior and strong engineering control over fabrication variability.
